An Oz Box is not only for code. It is an always-on machine of your own, in your own cloud account, where an AI agent can do
the routine work of a business: read, sort, draft, compare and report. It runs on a schedule or when you ask from chat. A
person approves anything that leaves the building, and every action is written to a log that shows if it was edited.

See [how to read the status](/use-cases#how-to-read-the-status). Everything here is **Preview** at most: the parts (scheduled
runs, chat, approvals, memory, skills, the permission engine and the audit log) are built and tested, but have not been run end
to end by a business on a real box yet. Someone technical installs Oz Box once; after that, day-to-day use is the dashboard
and chat.

**What the agent can reach.** Only what you give it: files you put on the box, and services you connect through declared
commands or a tool server, with keys it can use but never read ([a secure workspace](/use-cases/secure-agent-workspace)). Oz Box
ships no email, CRM or accounting connector of its own; you bring the one you use.

**Ground rules for every example:** the agent drafts and a person decides. Anything sent, published, submitted or paid goes
through an approval in the dashboard or chat.

## 1. Morning inbox and lead triage

**You want:** incoming enquiries sorted before you start work, with reply drafts and new leads listed.

```
ozbox schedule add --name inbox-triage --when "every weekday at 7am Lagos time" \
  --engine claude-code --box ops --prompt "Sort new enquiries, draft replies, list new leads" --results-to alert
```

In the dashboard: Schedules → New, then pick the box, the agent and the time in your own time zone.

**Underneath:** the run starts on the box at that time, reads the mailbox through the connector you declared, and writes its
drafts and the lead list to the run's history; the summary comes to you on the alert channel you chose.
**Limits:** Oz Box has no email connector; you connect your mail provider's API through a declared command or a tool server.
The agent never sends: replies go out only after you approve them.
**Status:** Preview.

## 2. A weekly business report, written for you

**You want:** a one-page summary of the week's numbers posted to your team chat every Monday.

```
ozbox schedule add --name weekly-report --when "every monday at 8am" --engine claude-code --box ops \
  --prompt "Pull last week's figures, write a one-page summary with three things to watch" --results-to alert
```

**Underneath:** the agent reads exports or APIs you declared, writes the summary, and the result is posted to the channel
you routed alerts to (Slack, Telegram, email).
**Limits:** the figures are only as good as the sources; the summary names each source and date so you can check it.
**Status:** Preview.

## 3. Competitor and market watch

**You want:** to know when a competitor changes their pricing or launches something, without checking their sites yourself.

```
ozbox schedule add --name market-watch --when "every friday at 4pm" --engine claude-code --box ops \
  --prompt "Check these pages, report only what changed since last week, with links" --results-to alert
```

**Underneath:** the agent fetches the pages through the tool host's browser tool, which only reads pages and only from hosts
you list in Permissions, and keeps last week's notes in its [memory](#underneath-memory-and-skills) to compare.
**Limits:** read-only fetches; pages that need a login or heavy scripts may not be readable.
**Status:** Preview.

## 4. Proposal and RFP first drafts

**You want:** a first draft of a proposal from the client's brief and your past proposals.

Put your past proposals and the brief in a folder on the box (upload them through the web IDE, `ozbox ide <box>`), then ask
from chat: *"Draft a proposal for the brief in /work/proposals/acme, in our usual structure."*

**Underneath:** the agent reads the files you gave it, follows a writing skill you approved (your structure and tone), and
saves a draft next to the brief.
**Limits:** it drafts; pricing and commitments are yours to set.
**Status:** Preview.

## 5. Customer support drafts

**You want:** answers to support questions drafted from your own help pages, checked by a person before they go out.

Approve a reply-drafting skill once (`ozbox skills show reply-drafter`, then `ozbox skills approve reply-drafter`), then start
runs from chat or a schedule.

**Underneath:** the bundled `reply-drafter` skill drafts from the sources you allow; every reply waits in the approvals inbox.
**Limits:** never sends on its own; a person approves each reply.
**Status:** Preview.

## 6. Meeting follow-ups

**You want:** a summary, the decisions, the action items and a follow-up email draft, from a meeting transcript.

Drop the transcript on the box and ask from chat: *"Summarise /work/meetings/2026-11-10.txt and draft the follow-up."*

**Underneath:** a single supervised run with a spend cap; the output is saved beside the transcript.
**Limits:** Oz Box does not record meetings; bring the transcript from your meeting tool.
**Status:** Preview.

## 7. Contract and renewal tracking

**You want:** renewal dates, notice periods and obligations pulled out of your contracts, and a reminder before each deadline.

Put the contracts in a folder on the box, run the extraction once, then schedule a weekly check that alerts you about anything
due in the next 60 days.

**Underneath:** the extraction is saved as a table on the box; the weekly schedule reads it and alerts you.
**Limits:** the agent can misread a clause; the table links each date to the page it came from so you can check.
**Status:** Preview.

## 8. Web portals and forms

**You want:** an agent that fills in supplier or government portals for you, and stops for your approval before submitting.

**Today:** the agent's browser tool reads pages; it cannot click through or submit forms. The box has a browser desktop you can
open yourself (`ozbox desktop <box>`) to do it by hand from any device.
**Status:** Planned (agent-driven form filling with an approval before submit).

## 9. Hiring pipeline support

**You want:** applications screened against your criteria and a shortlist, with interview scheduling.

Give the agent the applications and a written list of criteria, and ask for a ranked shortlist with the reason for each.

**Underneath:** a supervised run; the shortlist and reasons are saved for you to review.
**Limits:** the agent never decides; a person reviews every shortlist. Check your local rules on automated decisions in hiring
before relying on it.
**Status:** Preview.

## 10. A private assistant for a team

**You want:** one shared workspace where the whole team's agent knows your products, prices and tone, inside your own account.

One box per team, with team notes in memory (`ozbox memory add --scope project:ops --title … --body …`) and your approved
skills installed (`ozbox skills install <skill> --scope project:ops`). Team members talk to it from the dashboard or chat.

**Underneath:** the memory is plain files in git with a search index; agents can propose notes but only a person makes them
readable to agents. Roles decide who can use what.
**Limits:** everyone on the box shares one agent account on it; give a sensitive project its own box.
**Status:** Preview.

---

### Underneath: memory and skills

- **Memory** keeps facts between runs (your products, last week's findings) as plain notes you can read and edit. Agents
  propose notes; a person approves them.
- **Skills** are written instructions an agent follows (your proposal structure, your support tone). A skill works only at the
  exact version a person approved; any change needs approving again.
